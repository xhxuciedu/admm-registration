\begin{abstract}
Selecting the penalty and over-relaxation parameters of quadratic ADMM can
require numerical optimization of the spectral radius of a large iteration
operator. We identify a separable class for which this optimization reduces to
four scalar modes. When the joint modal spectrum is a Cartesian product of data
and regularizer curvatures with attained endpoint combinations, the oADMM modal
factor is coordinatewise monotone, so its worst magnitude is attained at four
endpoint combinations. The
conditional relaxation has a closed form, and the remaining penalty problem is
solved by a finite algebraic candidate set rather than a grid search, gradient
descent, or repeated spectral-radius calculation. Spatially varying
diffeomorphic registration is noncommuting and does not satisfy the exact
theorem globally. Local coefficient freezing recovers the separable structure,
while the rank-one optical-flow Hessian yields an inexpensive four-corner
parameter predictor. On 134 FIRE retinal pairs at $256\times256$, one-shot
tuning reduced median pairwise runtime by 34.4\% and inner iterations by 39.6\%
relative to a fixed pair selected on separate validation data, with essentially
unchanged landmark error and no observed nonpositive Jacobian. On ten CIMA
histology pairs, matched-protocol runtime decreased by 20.1\%. Optional
noncommuting rate bounds are rigorous but conservative and are not used by the
deployed selector.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} alternating direction method of multipliers;
parameter selection; spectral analysis; diffeomorphic image registration;
local Fourier analysis

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Alternating direction methods are attractive for large quadratic imaging
problems because each split subproblem can retain its natural computational
structure. In variational image registration, for example, one update is
pixelwise and the other is diagonal in a Fourier basis
\cite{Thorley2021,Song2024}. This advantage does not remove the sensitivity of
ADMM to its penalty $\rho$ and, for over-relaxed ADMM (oADMM), its relaxation
$\alpha$. Poor fixed values can dominate the cost of an otherwise efficient
solver.

Optimal parameter formulas are known for several quadratic classes
\cite{Ghadimi2015,Teixeira2016,GiselssonBoyd2017}. Song et al.\ treat a more
general linear-quadratic problem by mapping each trial penalty to an iteration
operator, evaluating its spectral radius, and numerically optimizing over the
penalty; relaxation is then selected conditionally \cite{Song2024}. The cost is
\emph{many trial values of $\rho$ times spectral-radius evaluation of a large
operator}. The penalty does not change during a fixed inner solve: the expense
is incurred while finding the fixed pair for that quadratic subproblem.

The central observation here applies to a separable quadratic split. Its oADMM
contraction on a mode depends on only two nonnegative curvatures, $h$ and $g$.
The resulting scalar function is monotone in each coordinate on a spectral
rectangle. Consequently, its worst magnitude is attained at the four corners.
This reduces a large spectral optimization to four rational branches. The
relaxation can be eliminated analytically, and a finite set of algebraic
candidates contains a global minimizer of the resulting four-corner objective
on a prescribed admissible penalty interval.

Real image registration does not possess this global separability: the
pixelwise image Hessian varies spatially, while the regularizer couples
neighboring pixels. We recover the exact modal model locally by freezing the
image coefficient. The rank-one optical-flow Hessian then collapses all local
curvature endpoints to two global values. After an $O(n)$ scan of the image
gradients, parameter optimization has only four scalar branches.
The conceptual sequence is therefore
\begin{align*}
\text{quadratic ADMM}&\longrightarrow\text{modal factor}
\longrightarrow\text{four corners}\\
&\longrightarrow\text{finite parameter selection}
\longrightarrow\text{noncommuting registration}.
\end{align*}

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a four-corner spectral theorem for quadratic oADMM with a
Cartesian-product joint modal spectrum;
\item a finite algebraic method for jointly selecting $\rho$ and $\alpha$ for
that model, without repeated iteration-matrix eigensolves;
\item a local-freezing extension to spatially varying problems and a rank-one
specialization that gives a one-shot registration predictor; and
\item matched-protocol FIRE and CIMA experiments showing reduced iterations and
runtime with essentially unchanged registration accuracy and no observed
nonpositive discrete Jacobian under the reported protocol.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\subsection{ADMM parameter selection}
Scaled ADMM and practical residual criteria were systematized by Boyd et al.\
\cite{Boyd2011}. Ghadimi et al.\ derived optimal parameters for several
quadratic classes \cite{Ghadimi2015}; Teixeira et al.\ studied parameter
selection, scaling, and preconditioning in distributed quadratic programs
\cite{Teixeira2016}; and Giselsson and Boyd developed tight metric-dependent
contraction bounds \cite{GiselssonBoyd2017}. Adaptive alternatives include
residual balancing and Barzilai--Borwein (BB) spectral updates
\cite{Wohlberg2017,Xu2017}.

\subsection{Spectral and over-relaxed ADMM}
Song et al.\ optimize ADMM and oADMM parameters for general linear-quadratic
problems \cite{Song2024}. Their pipeline is
\[
\rho\ \longmapsto\ E_\rho\ \longmapsto\ \spr(E_\rho)\
\longmapsto\ \text{numerical optimization over }\rho,
\]
with an optimal or closed-form relaxation conditional on $\rho$. In contrast,
the present separable model maps four spectral endpoints to four rational
branches and then to a finite algebraic candidate set. This distinction concerns
parameter-selection overhead and structure, not changing $\rho$ during inner
ADMM iterations. We do not claim superiority to full-resolution direct spectral
optimization without a direct experiment at that resolution.

\subsection{Fourier and local Fourier analysis}
Fourier analysis has long characterized stationary iterations for elliptic
problems \cite{ChanElman1989}, and local Fourier analysis (LFA) is a standard
tool for multigrid design and parameter optimization
\cite{WienandsJoppich2005}. Translation-invariant periodic problems admit exact
Fourier decompositions; local and nonstandard analyses can remain informative
for random or discontinuous coefficients \cite{Rodrigo2017,Kumar2019}. Here the
stationary map is an ADMM error iteration, and local freezing converts a
matrix-valued optical-flow coefficient into a separable patch model.

\subsection{Variational image registration}
DARTEL \cite{Ashburner2007}, diffeomorphic demons
\cite{Vercauteren2009}, and large-deformation optimal-control formulations
\cite{MangBiros2015} are representative optimization-based registration
methods. Thorley et al.\ compose nonstationary velocity updates and solve
regularized convex subproblems with accelerated ADMM on GPUs
\cite{Thorley2021}. Song et al.\ use a related quadratic model in their spectral
oADMM study \cite{Song2024}. Learning-based methods broaden the current
landscape \cite{Hering2022}, but the present work concerns numerical parameter
selection for the linearized variational subproblem.

\section{Quadratic ADMM and Reduced Error Dynamics}\label{sec:quadratic}
\subsection{Quadratic splitting}
Consider
\begin{equation}
\min_{v,w}\;
\underbrace{\tfrac12v^\top Hv+b^\top v}_{f(v)}
+\underbrace{\tfrac12w^\top Gw}_{r(w)}
\quad\text{subject to}\quad v-w=0,
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
where $v,w,b\in\R^N$ and $H,G\in\R^{N\times N}$ are symmetric positive
semidefinite matrices. The linear term $b$ determines the fixed point but
disappears from homogeneous error propagation.

\subsection{Standard and over-relaxed ADMM}
With scaled dual variable $u$ and penalty $\rho>0$, standard ADMM is
\begin{align}
(H+\rho\Id)v^{k+1}&=-b+\rho(w^k-u^k),\label{eq:vupdate}\\
(G+\rho\Id)w^{k+1}&=\rho(v^{k+1}+u^k),\label{eq:wupdate}\\
u^{k+1}&=u^k+v^{k+1}-w^{k+1}.\label{eq:uupdate}
\end{align}
For over-relaxed ADMM, abbreviated \emph{oADMM}, define
\begin{equation}
\widehat v^{k+1}=\alpha v^{k+1}+(1-\alpha)w^k,
\qquad 0<\alpha\le2,
\label{eq:relaxation}
\end{equation}
and replace $v^{k+1}$ by $\widehat v^{k+1}$ in
\eqref{eq:wupdate} and the dual update.

\subsection{Reduced Cayley representation}
Define
\[
C_H(\rho)=(H-\rho\Id)(H+\rho\Id)^{-1},\qquad
C_G(\rho)=(G-\rho\Id)(G+\rho\Id)^{-1}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Reduced Cayley error operator]\label{prop:reduced}
The nonzero spectra of the oADMM state map and
\begin{equation}
E_{\rho,\alpha}
=\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\Id
+\frac{\alpha}{2}C_H(\rho)C_G(\rho).
\label{eq:Ealpha}
\end{equation}
coincide, including algebraic multiplicity; the remaining state-map
eigenvalues are zero. Thus the asymptotic factor is
$\spr(E_{\rho,\alpha})$, where $\spr(\,\cdot\,)$ denotes the spectral radius
of a matrix or linear operator.
\end{proposition}
Appendix~\ref{app:reduced} gives the reduction. It is supporting machinery:
the parameter reduction below comes from the modal dependence of
\eqref{eq:Ealpha}.

\begin{theorem}[Basic contraction]\label{thm:contraction}
If $H,G\succeq0$ and $H+G\succ0$, then for every $\rho>0$,
$\kappa_\rho:=\norm{C_H(\rho)C_G(\rho)}_2<1$. Consequently
\[
\spr(E_{\rho,\alpha})
\le 1-\frac{\alpha}{2}(1-\kappa_\rho)<1,
\]
for $0<\alpha\le2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $A\succeq0$, the Cayley eigenvalue
$(\lambda-\rho)/(\lambda+\rho)$ has magnitude at most one, with equality only
for $\lambda=0$. Equality in
$\norm{C_HC_Gx}\le\norm{C_Gx}\le\norm{x}$ would imply
$x\in\ker G$ and $C_Gx=-x\in\ker H$, contradicting
$H+G\succ0$. Compactness gives strict inequality; the displayed estimate
follows from \eqref{eq:Ealpha}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Nullspaces.}
If a nonzero vector lies in $\ker H\cap\ker G$, then
$C_HC_Gx=x$ and $E_{\rho,\alpha}x=x$ for every admissible parameter pair.
Strict contraction therefore requires $H+G\succ0$, or restriction to the
identifiable subspace. In the four-corner registration model,
$\nu=\gamma=0$ creates the $(h,g)=(0,0)$ mode; the selector then requires
positive damping, a coercive boundary/regularization condition, or explicit
removal of that common nullspace.

\subsection{Scalar modal factor}
When a vector is a common eigenvector of $H$ and $G$, with eigenvalues $h$ and
$g$, \eqref{eq:Ealpha} acts by
\begin{align}
e(h,g;\rho,\alpha)
&=1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
+\frac{\alpha}{2}
\frac{h-\rho}{h+\rho}\frac{g-\rho}{g+\rho}\label{eq:modal}\\
&=1-\alpha+\alpha\theta(h,g;\rho),\nonumber\\
\theta(h,g;\rho)&=
\frac{\rho^2+hg}{(\rho+h)(\rho+g)}.\label{eq:theta}
\end{align}
For nonnegative $h$ and $g$, $0\le\theta\le1$. The paired values $(h,g)$,
not the marginal spectra separately, determine the true modal factor.

\section{Four-Corner Spectral Tuning}\label{sec:fourcorner}
\subsection{Separable commuting modal structure}
\begin{definition}[Cartesian-product joint modal spectrum]\label{def:cartesian}
A quadratic split is \emph{separable} on modal sets
$\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal G$ if $H$ and $G$ are simultaneously diagonalizable
and their attained joint modal spectrum is
\[
\{(h,g): Hx=hx,\ Gx=gx\text{ for a common mode }x\ne0\}
=\mathcal H\times\mathcal G.
\]
For example,
$H_{\rm sep}=I_m\otimes H_c$ and
$G_{\rm sep}=G_s\otimes I_d$ have modes
$\phi_\xi\otimes z_j$ and pairs $(h_j,g(\xi))$, where
$H_cz_j=h_jz_j$, $G_s\phi_\xi=g(\xi)\phi_\xi$,
$H_c\in\R^{d\times d}$ acts on components,
$G_s\in\R^{m\times m}$ acts on spatial degrees of freedom, and $I_m,I_d$ are
the corresponding identity matrices.
\end{definition}
The Cartesian-product condition is stronger than commutativity. It asserts
that data and regularizer modes combine independently.

\subsection{Four-corner theorem}
For fixed $\rho>0$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial h}
=\frac{\rho(g-\rho)}{(g+\rho)(h+\rho)^2},\qquad
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial g}
=\frac{\rho(h-\rho)}{(h+\rho)(g+\rho)^2}.
\label{eq:theta_derivatives}
\end{equation}
The sign of the first derivative is independent of $h$, and the sign of the
second is independent of $g$. Thus $\theta$ is coordinatewise monotone on any
rectangle.

\begin{theorem}[Four-Corner Spectral Theorem]\label{thm:fourcorner}
Suppose the joint modal spectrum is the Cartesian product
$\mathcal H\times\mathcal G$, where
$\mathcal H\subset[h_-,h_+]$ and
$\mathcal G\subset[g_-,g_+]$, with
$0\le h_-\le h_+$ and $0\le g_-\le g_+$. If all four endpoint combinations
are attained,
then
\begin{equation}
\spr(E_{\rho,\alpha})=
\Ufour(\rho,\alpha):=
\max_{\substack{h\in\{h_-,h_+\}\\g\in\{g_-,g_+\}}}
|e(h,g;\rho,\alpha)|
\label{eq:fourcorner}
\end{equation}
for every $\rho>0$ and $0<\alpha\le2$. If only the interval enclosure is known,
the right-hand side remains a valid rectangular upper envelope, but equality
with the true spectral radius need not hold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:theta_derivatives} makes the extrema of $\theta$ on the
rectangle occur at corners. The map
$t\mapsto|1-\alpha+\alpha t|$ is convex, so its maximum over the interval
between those extrema also occurs at an endpoint and hence at a corner.
Cartesian-product structure and attained endpoint combinations make those
corners actual modes, proving equality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optimal relaxation for fixed penalty}
Let the four corner values satisfy
\[
a(\rho)=\min_c\theta_c(\rho),\qquad
b(\rho)=\max_c\theta_c(\rho).
\]
The four-corner objective is
\[
\max\{|1-\alpha+\alpha a|,\ |1-\alpha+\alpha b|\}.
\]
Equioscillation of the two endpoint factors gives the unconstrained optimizer;
if $(a,b)\ne(1,1)$, accounting for $0<\alpha\le2$ yields
\begin{equation}
\alpha^\star(\rho)=
\min\left\{2,\frac{2}{2-a(\rho)-b(\rho)}\right\}.
\label{eq:alpha_star}
\end{equation}
After eliminating $\alpha$, the reduced objective is
\begin{align}
\widehat U(\rho)&=\frac{b-a}{2-a-b}
&&\text{if }a+b\le1,\label{eq:reduced_objective}\\
\widehat U(\rho)&=2b-1
&&\text{if }a+b\ge1.\nonumber
\end{align}
In the degenerate case $a=b=1$, the objective equals one for every admissible
$\alpha$; any relaxation may be selected, but the mode is not contractive.

\subsection{Finite algebraic penalty selection}
First deduplicate identical rational corner branches $\theta_c(\rho)$. On a
compact admissible interval
$\mathcal I=[\rho_-,\rho_+]\subset(0,\infty)$, changes in the active minimum or
maximum occur only at pairwise branch intersections.

\begin{theorem}[Finite penalty candidate set]\label{thm:finite}
A global minimizer of \eqref{eq:reduced_objective} over $\mathcal I$ is contained
in the finite set formed by:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\rho_-$ and $\rho_+$;
\item positive pairwise branch-intersection roots in $\mathcal I$;
\item roots of $a(\rho)+b(\rho)=1$ for every active branch pair; and
\item stationary roots of each active rational expression in
\eqref{eq:reduced_objective}.
\end{enumerate}
Identically zero intersection, transition, or stationarity polynomials are
discarded; a constant objective piece is represented by its interval
endpoints. Thus the candidate set remains finite when endpoints coincide.
After positive denominators are cleared, the respective polynomial degrees are
at most three, four, and six. Evaluating \eqref{eq:alpha_star} and
\eqref{eq:fourcorner} on this set---using any admissible $\alpha$ in the
degenerate $a=b=1$ case---returns a global minimizer of the four-corner
objective over $\mathcal I\times(0,2]$.
\end{theorem}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:finite}. The result replaces a continuum of
trial penalties by a constant number of polynomial roots:
\[
\boxed{\text{no grid search}+\text{no gradient descent in }\rho
+\text{no repeated spectral-radius calculation}.}
\]

\subsection{Arbitrary commuting operators versus Cartesian products}
Three cases must be distinguished.
\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{Case A:}] For a separable commuting split with attained endpoint combinations,
\eqref{eq:fourcorner} is the true spectral radius, and finite tuning is globally
optimal for that model over $\mathcal I\times(0,2]$.
\item[\textbf{Case B:}] If $HG=GH$ but the paired spectrum is only
$\{(h_i,g_i)\}_i$, the exact factor is
$\max_i|e(h_i,g_i;\rho,\alpha)|$. The four corners of the marginal spectral
rectangle can be artificial and provide an envelope rather than an equality.
\item[\textbf{Case C:}] If $HG\ne GH$, no global scalar modal decomposition follows. Four-corner
tuning is a surrogate or predictor unless a separate argument supplies a
bound.
\end{description}

\section{Spatially Varying and Noncommuting Problems}\label{sec:noncommuting}
\subsection{Why global modal analysis fails}
Let $H=\diag(H_1,\ldots,H_n)$ vary by location and let
$G=G_0\otimes I_d$ couple neighboring sites. In block form,
\[
[H,G]_{ij}=(G_0)_{ij}(H_i-H_j),
\]
so $HG\ne GH$ whenever coupled sites have different data blocks. The exact
factor is then
\[
R_{\rm true}(\rho,\alpha)=\spr(E_{\rho,\alpha}),
\]
not a maximum of the scalar factors in \eqref{eq:modal}.

\subsection{Local freezing and patchwise commuting approximation}
Let nonoverlapping patches $\{\Omega_r\}$ partition the spatial grid. On
$\Omega_r$, freeze the coefficient at a symmetric positive semidefinite
representative block $H_r$. Let $G_r$ be a symmetric patch regularizer with a
specified closure, such as periodic or cosine, and define
\[
\widetilde H_r=I_{|\Omega_r|}\otimes H_r,\qquad
\widetilde G_r=G_r\otimes I_d.
\]
Then $\widetilde H_r\widetilde G_r=\widetilde G_r\widetilde H_r$. If
$H_rz_j=h_{r,j}z_j$ and $G_r\phi_\xi=g_r(\xi)\phi_\xi$, the legitimate joint
modes are $\phi_\xi\otimes z_j$ and their exact local factors are
$e(h_{r,j},g_r(\xi);\rho,\alpha)$.

\subsection{Global minimax over local modes}
One global pair is selected across all patches; patches do not receive separate
ADMM penalties. With attained patch symbol endpoints $g_r^-$ and $g_r^+$, set
\[
\mathcal C=\bigcup_{r,j}
\{(h_{r,j},g_r^-),(h_{r,j},g_r^+)\}.
\]
The patch-surrogate objective is exactly
\begin{equation}
\Upatch(\rho,\alpha)=
\max_{(h,g)\in\mathcal C}|e(h,g;\rho,\alpha)|.
\label{eq:patch_objective}
\end{equation}
The same finite branch argument produces a minimax pair over all local models
and $\mathcal I\times(0,2]$.

\subsection{Optional variable-coefficient rate bounds}
Rigorous noncommuting bounds may be evaluated after tuning. For example, if
$\widetilde H=\bigoplus_r\widetilde H_r$ and
$\widetilde G=\bigoplus_r\widetilde G_r$ are the full-size direct-sum
surrogates on the patch partition, and
\[
\delta_H=\norm{H-\widetilde H}_2,\qquad
\delta_G=\norm{G-\widetilde G}_2,
\]
then the Cayley resolvent identity gives
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm true}(\rho,\alpha)
\le \Upatch(\rho,\alpha)
+\frac{\alpha}{\rho}(\delta_H+\delta_G).
\label{eq:additive_bound}
\end{equation}
Each direct-sum block is simultaneously diagonalizable, so
$\widetilde E_{\rho,\alpha}$ is normal and
$\norm{\widetilde E_{\rho,\alpha}}_2=\Upatch(\rho,\alpha)$ when the local
symbol endpoints used in \eqref{eq:patch_objective} are attained. Cross-patch
regularizer couplings removed by the direct sum are included in $\delta_G$.
This bound requires no commutativity of the full operators but is conservative.
It and the sharper block-Gershgorin, signed-rectangle, Perron-comparison, and
truncated-kernel constructions are detailed in the supplementary information.
None is used during deployed parameter selection.

\section{Application to Diffeomorphic Image Registration}\label{sec:registration}
\subsection{Linearized registration model}
Let $n$ grid points lie in dimension $d\in\{2,3\}$, let $\Delta x$ be the grid
spacing, and let $q_i=\nabla I(x_i)$. At the current linearization,
$v_i\in\R^d$ is the velocity increment at $x_i$ and $I_t(x_i)$ is the scalar
brightness residual. A damped linearized optical-flow subproblem can be written
\begin{equation}
\min_v\;
\frac{\mu}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n(q_i^\top v_i+I_t(x_i))^2
+\frac{\nu}{2}\norm{v}_2^2+\frac12v^\top Gv,
\label{eq:registration}
\end{equation}
where $\mu>0$ weights data fidelity, $\nu\ge0$ is an optional damping weight,
and $G\succeq0$ is the regularization Hessian. Setting $\nu=0$ gives the
undamped model.
Splitting a copy $w=v$ places \eqref{eq:registration} in
\eqref{eq:split}.

\subsection{Rank-one data Hessian}
The data Hessian is
\begin{equation}
H=\diag(H_1,\ldots,H_n),\qquad
H_i=\nu I_d+\mu q_iq_i^\top.
\label{eq:registration_H}
\end{equation}
If $q_i\ne0$, then $H_i$ has eigenvalue $\nu$ with multiplicity $d-1$ and
eigenvalue $\nu+\mu\norm{q_i}^2$ along $q_i$. If $q_i=0$, all $d$ eigenvalues
equal $\nu$. Therefore the attained global curvature endpoints are
\begin{equation}
h_-=\nu,\qquad h_+=\nu+\mu\max_i\norm{q_i}^2.
\label{eq:h_endpoints}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sobolev regularizer and symbol}
The componentwise regularizer has
\[
G=G_0\otimes I_d.
\]
For a first-order periodic Sobolev discretization with stiffness $\beta>0$ and
zeroth-order weight $\gamma\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
g(\xi)=\gamma+
\frac{4\beta}{(\Delta x)^2}
\sum_{\ell=1}^d\sin^2\left(\frac{\xi_\ell}{2}\right).
\label{eq:sobolev_symbol}
\end{equation}
Thus $g_-=\gamma$. When every Nyquist-frequency corner is present, as on the
even periodic grids used here,
$g_+=\gamma+4\beta d/(\Delta x)^2$; otherwise the exact maximum over the
discrete frequency set is used. Higher-order powers use the corresponding
powered endpoints.

\subsection{Registration-specific four-corner predictor}
For a registration patch, choose a representative gradient $\bar q_r$ and set
\[
H_r=\nu I_d+\mu\bar q_r\bar q_r^\top.
\]
Local freezing at $H_r$ recovers the separable tensor-product structure. The
deployed four-corner surrogate uses the family of pixelwise coefficient freezes
$H_r=H_i$; equivalently, the representative set includes every $q_i$. Hence
$h_+$ in \eqref{eq:h_endpoints} is attained, and the rank-one endpoint collapse
leaves the four branches
\[
(h_-,g_-),\quad(h_-,g_+),\quad(h_+,g_-),\quad(h_+,g_+).
\]
If only mean or median patch representatives are retained, these four branches
remain a rectangular envelope but need not equal the patch spectrum.
For these attained pixelwise endpoints, define
$R_{\rm 4C}:=\Ufour$ and
\[
(\rho_{\rm 4C},\alpha_{\rm 4C})
\in\argmin_{\rho\in\mathcal I,\ 0<\alpha\le2}
R_{\rm 4C}(\rho,\alpha).
\]
\begin{corollary}[Rank-one frozen-registration reduction]
\label{cor:registration_fourcorner}
Assume $d\ge2$, the coefficient-frozen family contains every $H_i$, and the
common regularizer spectrum attains $g_-$ and $g_+$. Also assume
$\nu+\gamma>0$, or remove the common null mode on an explicitly identified
quotient subspace. Then the worst contraction factor
over that family equals $R_{\rm 4C}$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} returns a
global minimizer of this frozen separable surrogate over
$\mathcal I\times(0,2]$. For the assembled
variable-coefficient operator, the same pair is a predictor rather than a
generally optimal pair.
\end{corollary}
\begin{remark}
The four-corner reduction is exact for the separable commuting surrogate; the
resulting parameter pair is therefore globally optimal over
$\mathcal I\times(0,2]$ for that surrogate, but serves as a predictor of the
optimal parameters for the full variable-coefficient registration iteration.
\end{remark}
In general, the selected pair need not belong to
\[
\argmin_{\rho\in\mathcal I,\ 0<\alpha\le2}
R_{\rm true}(\rho,\alpha).
\]
The predictor ignores global orientation interactions between spatially varying
rank-one blocks; the experiments assess its practical transfer.

\subsection{One-shot deployment policy}
\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: One-Shot Four-Corner ADMM Tuning}
\emph{Inputs:} initial image gradients $\{q_i\}$, weights $\mu,\nu$, regularizer
parameters and grid, and admissible penalty interval $\mathcal I$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item From the initial image gradient, compute
$h_-=\nu$ and $h_+=\nu+\mu\max_i\norm{q_i}^2$.
\item Compute $g_-$ and $g_+$ analytically from the regularizer and grid.
\item Form the four endpoint pairs and their rational functions
$\theta_c(\rho)$.
\item On the admissible interval, generate the finite algebraic penalty
candidate set of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}.
\item At each candidate compute $a(\rho)$, $b(\rho)$, and
$\alpha^\star(\rho)$ (or an admissible default in the degenerate case); select
the pair minimizing $R_{\rm 4C}$.
\item Run inner oADMM with this fixed pair.
\item In one-shot deployment, reuse the pair at later outer registration
iterations and pyramid levels unless an explicit retuning policy is enabled.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

An \emph{inner ADMM iteration} solves one quadratic problem with fixed
$H,G,b$, so its optimal fixed parameters do not change during that solve. An
\emph{outer registration iteration} updates the deformation and relinearizes;
the warped image gradients and $H$ may then change. The one-shot policy accepts
this model drift to avoid repeated tuning. The reported ablation tests
per-pyramid-level retuning, not separate retuning at every outer
Gauss--Newton iteration.

\section{Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
The reference two-dimensional solver uses a three-level Gaussian pyramid,
spatial image gradients, Gauss--Newton optical-flow linearization,
Sherman--Morrison solves for the rank-one data blocks, and FFT solves for the
Sobolev regularizer. Velocity increments are composed with the displacement. A
backtracking line search requires objective decrease and a discrete Jacobian
above $0.05$. Pyramid transfer is audited and, if needed, its
nontranslational component is halved until the same floor holds. All methods in
a comparison share initialization, objective, stopping tolerances, line search,
pyramid, and topology safeguards.

The spectral tests use direct eigendecomposition only on tractable systems.
The controlled spectral studies use
$\mathcal I=[10^{-2},10^2]$; the real-image selector and the $8\times8$
spectral diagnostic use $\mathcal I=[10^{-4},10^3]$. Implementations use
$\alpha\in[10^{-6},2]$ as a numerical realization of $0<\alpha\le2$.
The constant-coefficient test uses periodic $n\times n$ grids,
$(h_-,h_+)=(0.2,2.3)$, and first-order regularization with
$(\beta,\gamma)=(0.2,0.1)$. Controlled noncommuting tests separate periodic
magnitude variation, smooth orientation variation, a sharp orientation
interface, and Gaussian-smoothed image gradients on $3\times3$ grids.
For these tests, let $R_{\rm act}$ be the true spectral radius at the selected
parameters, $R_{\rm ora}$ the radius at numerically oracle-optimized
parameters, and $U$ a rigorous enclosure evaluated at the selected pair. We
report the parameter-selection gap
\[
\frac{R_{\rm act}-R_{\rm ora}}{R_{\rm ora}}
\]
and the enclosure gap $(U-R_{\rm act})/R_{\rm act}$. Thus the former measures
the spectral performance lost by parameter selection, not the Euclidean
distance between two parameter vectors.

The real-image pilot uses a public-domain retinal image and an unrestricted
immunohistochemistry image under held-out smooth known deformations. CIMA uses
the five differently stained sections of the public lung-lesion-3 sample and
all ten unordered pairs \cite{Borovec2018}, resized to $256^2$ after a common
structural-channel preprocessing. FIRE uses all 134 landmarked pairs (14 A, 49
P, and 71 S) at $256^2$ \cite{HernandezMatas2017}. Landmarks are used only for
evaluation. Timings use one CPU BLAS/OpenMP thread. Reproducibility details,
baseline safeguards, seeds, and certificate protocols are in the supplement.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Exact four-corner validation}
This first experiment is a controlled synthetic verification of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fourcorner}. We form a
constant-coefficient quadratic split on small periodic $n\times n$ grids with
prescribed data-curvature endpoints $(h_-,h_+)=(0.2,2.3)$---corresponding to
$(\nu,\mu\max_i\norm{q_i}^2)=(0.2,2.1)$---and a first-order Sobolev regularizer
with $(\beta,\gamma)=(0.2,0.1)$. Because the data coefficient is spatially
constant, the operators commute and the joint modal spectrum is exactly the
Cartesian product of the two curvature intervals with the discrete regularizer
symbol; no image intensity or landmark data enter this test.

The finite algebraic selector returned
$(\rho,\alpha)=(0.543512,1.898152)$ on every grid. Table~\ref{tab:constant}
compares the four-corner envelope $U_{\rm 4C}$ with the spectral radius of the
full reduced operator $E_{\rho,\alpha}$, obtained by direct
eigendecomposition. The two agree to machine precision, confirming exact
four-corner equality for this separable model. Panel~(a) of
Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral} displays the same agreement.

\subsection{Controlled noncommuting tests}
This experiment remains synthetic: it asks how well the four-corner predictor
transfers when the data Hessian varies spatially, so that $HG\ne GH$ and the
exact four-corner equality no longer holds. On $3\times3$ periodic grids we
constructed ten variable-coefficient optical-flow Hessians from magnitude
variation, smooth orientation variation, one sharp orientation interface, and
Gaussian-smoothed random gradients; nine cases are smooth. For each case we
compared the predictor pair with a numerical spectral oracle for the true
reduced operator and evaluated optional rate certificates at the selected
parameters. No registration images or landmarks are used here.

Across the ten cases, the predictor had a median relative parameter gap of
$0.057$ overall and $0.027$ on the smooth cases, whereas the single
abrupt-interface case had a gap of $0.193$; no case collapsed to the parameter
boundary. At the selected parameters, the median absolute relative discrepancy
between $R_{\rm 4C}$ and $R_{\rm true}$ was $1.4\times10^{-15}$ and the
maximum was $2.8\times10^{-3}$. The rigorous Perron comparison bound was below
one in all ten cases, but its median enclosure gap was $1.29$. In a separate
18-case comparison of signed enclosures, every enclosure contained the exact
radius. The Perron rectangle was the tightest of the signed constructions,
with median enclosure and parameter gaps $0.595$ and $0.386$, but remained too
conservative for online tuning. The detailed certificate table and scaling
study are in the supplement.

The archived controlled runs distinguish magnitude, smooth orientation,
abrupt-interface, and smoothed-random regimes, but did not store a normalized
commutator magnitude. Adding
$\norm{[H,G]}_2/(\norm H_2\norm G_2)$ as a horizontal-axis diagnostic is
therefore a TODO rather than an unreported result.

\paragraph{Controlled real-content pilot.}
Before the public landmark benchmarks, we ran a small end-to-end registration
pilot on real image content with known synthetic warps. A public-domain retinal
image and an unrestricted immunohistochemistry image were each subjected to
five smooth known deformations; cases 1--2 selected the external fixed pair
$(\rho,\alpha)=(0.1,1)$, and held-out cases 3--5 provided six
image--deformation tests. The task is ordinary linearized diffeomorphic
registration under the shared solver protocol, measured by inner iterations,
runtime, intensity MSE, and discrete Jacobians rather than landmarks. The
one-shot predictor reduced median inner iterations by 44.4\% and median total
time by 41.7\% (bootstrap 95\% interval $[40.9,42.6]$\%) relative to that
validation-selected fixed pair. Selection consumed 1.58\% of proposed-method
runtime, median relative MSE change was $-0.0068$\%, and none of the six
tested cases had a nonpositive Jacobian. This two-image pilot is a solver
check rather than a landmark benchmark.

\subsection{Direct spectral optimization comparison}
This diagnostic uses the CIMA histology pairs below, but replaces four-corner
tuning by a Song-style spectral search on a coarse surrogate. For each pair,
the exact variable-coefficient reduced operator was formed on an $8\times8$
downsampled grid, $\rho$ was optimized numerically, and the resulting pair was
transferred to the full $256^2$ solve. At each trial penalty, if
$\{\lambda_j(\rho)\}$ are the eigenvalues of $C_H(\rho)C_G(\rho)$, the exact
conditional relaxation was computed as
\[
\alpha_\rho\in\argmin_{10^{-6}\le\alpha\le2}
\max_j\left|1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lambda_j(\rho)\right|.
\]
The question is therefore one of tuning overhead and coarse-model transfer,
not of superiority to full-resolution Song optimization. The spectral search
required a median 97 spectral-objective evaluations and $2.18$ seconds of
tuning. Median full-solve iterations were 251, compared with 210 for
four-corner tuning, and total times were $4.39$ and $2.02$ seconds,
respectively, with indistinguishable TRE.

\subsection{CIMA histology benchmark}
We next turn to real multimodal histology registration. The public
lung-lesion-3 sample from the CIMA landmark repository \cite{Borovec2018}
comprises five differently stained sections and all ten unordered pairs.
Images supplied at 5\% scale were converted to a common structural channel and
resized to $256^2$; landmarks supplied at 50\% scale were rescaled for
evaluation only. The problem is diffeomorphic registration of these pairs under
the shared pyramid solver, comparing one-shot four-corner oADMM tuning with
fixed-parameter, residual-balancing, and BB-proxy baselines on target
registration error (TRE), iterations, runtime, and discrete Jacobians.

The fixed comparator $(\rho,\alpha)=(0.1,1)$ was selected on the separate
real-content pilot and frozen before CIMA. Across five order-alternated
repetitions of all ten pairs (50 paired runs), four-corner tuning reduced
median total runtime by 20.1\% (bootstrap 95\% interval 19.4--20.7\%), solver
time by 23.5\%, and iterations by 30.1\%. Selection used 1.16\% of proposed
runtime. Median TRE was $7.33$ pixels (2.02\% of the image diagonal) and
differed by less than $3\times10^{-5}$ pixels from the fixed comparator. The
median landmark-error improvement relative to the unregistered pairs was
36.5\%. No global or interior nonpositive Jacobian was observed. One difficult
stain pair retained TRE above 22 pixels, identifying initialization and
cross-stain representation rather than inner ADMM convergence as the main
failure mode.

\subsection{FIRE retinal benchmark}
The main end-to-end study uses the public FIRE fundus registration benchmark
\cite{HernandezMatas2017}: all 134 landmarked pairs (14~A, 49~P, and 71~S),

diffeomorphic registration under identical phase-correlation initialization,

One-shot four-corner tuning is compared with the external fixed pair, residual

Across all pairs, median iterations were 245 for one-shot four-corner tuning

paired reduction of 51.7\%. Median TRE differed from the fixed pair by

\subsection{One-shot reuse ablation}
This ablation stays within FIRE but isolates the parameter-reuse policy. On a
balanced 18-pair FIRE subset we compared the deployed one-shot policy---one
pair from the initial full-resolution gradient, reused at every pyramid
level---with retuning at every pyramid level. The question is whether fewer
inner iterations from retuning reduce end-to-end runtime once tuning cost is
included. Retuning reduced median inner iterations from 243 to 222 but
increased median total time from $2.651$ to $3.895$ seconds; median tuning
time also increased from $0.030$ to $0.110$ seconds. The archived aggregate
timings do not isolate the remainder of the increase, but they establish that
fewer iterations did not imply lower end-to-end runtime in this per-level
pipeline. The reported deployment policy therefore reuses one pair;
per-outer-iteration retuning was not separately tested.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Why four corners work}
The reduction follows from two facts: the separable model supplies independent
data and regularizer modal coordinates, and the sign of each partial derivative
in \eqref{eq:theta_derivatives} is independent of the differentiated
coordinate. Convexity after the affine relaxation map then preserves endpoint
attainment. This argument is independent of image size.

\subsection{Exact model optimum versus full-problem prediction}
Exact surrogate optimization is not exact variable-coefficient optimization.
The local frozen model has a Cartesian-product spectrum, but full registration
generally has $HG\ne GH$. The practical pair is therefore surrogate-optimal and
predictive for the full iteration. Its strong empirical performance suggests
that endpoint curvature information captures useful scale balance, especially
for smooth coefficients, but it is not a theorem about the minimizer of
$R_{\rm true}$.

\subsection{Comparison with Song et al.}
Song et al.\ address a more general problem class \cite{Song2024}. Their
parameter optimization pays for repeated spectral-radius evaluation while
finding $\rho$; neither their method nor ours requires changing $\rho$ during a
fixed inner solve. Four-corner tuning avoids this search under stronger
separable assumptions and becomes a predictor outside them. The $8\times8$
diagnostic quantifies overhead and transfer only.

\subsection{Limitations}
Local freezing is approximate, and the rank-one endpoint collapse omits
orientation interactions. The optional noncommuting certificates discard
directional cancellation and are conservative. CIMA contains one five-image
tissue set at reduced resolution; FIRE is evaluated at $256^2$ under a CPU
protocol and against an external fixed comparator, residual balancing, and a
safeguarded BB proxy. The BB proxy does not reproduce every adaptive ADMM
variant. The direct true-spectral-radius regret experiment remains TODO.
Finally, the theory describes each convex linearized subproblem, not global
convergence of the outer nonlinear diffeomorphic registration scheme.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
For quadratic oADMM with an attained Cartesian-product joint modal spectrum,
the worst contraction is exactly one of four endpoint factors. Conditional
relaxation and a finite algebraic penalty set then give search-free joint
tuning. Spatially varying registration is noncommuting, but local freezing and
its rank-one Hessian yield an inexpensive one-shot predictor. The FIRE and CIMA
results show substantial iteration and runtime reductions at matched accuracy
and observed topology. Future work should quantify direct spectral-radius
regret at tractable resolutions, extend the method to volumetric registration,
and tighten optional noncommuting bounds.

\section{Proof of the Reduced Cayley Representation}\label{app:reduced}
Set $P_H=\rho(H+\rho\Id)^{-1}$ and
$P_G=\rho(G+\rho\Id)^{-1}$. For standard ADMM, the homogeneous state map is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}w^{k+1}\\u^{k+1}\end{bmatrix}
=
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}P_G\\\Id-P_G\end{bmatrix}}_{A}
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}P_H&\Id-P_H\end{bmatrix}}_{B}
\begin{bmatrix}w^k\\u^k\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The nonzero eigenvalues of $AB$ equal those of
\[
BA=P_HP_G+(\Id-P_H)(\Id-P_G).
\]
Since $P_H=(\Id-C_H)/2$ and $P_G=(\Id-C_G)/2$,
\[
BA=\tfrac12(\Id+C_HC_G).
\]
Inserting \eqref{eq:relaxation} makes the reduced relaxed map
$(1-\alpha)\Id+\alpha BA$, which is \eqref{eq:Ealpha}.

\section{Proof Details for the Four-Corner Theorem}\label{app:corner}
For fixed $g$,
\[
\theta(h,g;\rho)=
\frac{\rho^2+hg}{(\rho+g)(\rho+h)}
\]
has derivative given by \eqref{eq:theta_derivatives}, whose sign is that of
$g-\rho$ and is constant in $h$. The analogous statement holds after
interchanging $h$ and $g$. Repeated one-dimensional endpoint maximization
therefore puts both the minimum and maximum of $\theta$ at rectangle corners.
Because every value of $\theta$ lies between those extrema and absolute value
of an affine function is convex, no interior value can exceed both endpoint
images. Equality with the operator radius additionally requires that the
maximizing endpoint pair be an actual common mode. Definition~\ref{def:cartesian}
and attained endpoints guarantee all four such modes.

\section{Proof of Finite Algebraic Selection}\label{app:finite}
Write
\[
\theta_c(\rho)=\frac{\rho^2+h_cg_c}
{(\rho+h_c)(\rho+g_c)}.
\]
Identical rational branches are first deduplicated. Every denominator is
positive for $\rho>0$. Pairwise equality of two distinct branches,
a transition equality $\theta_i+\theta_j=1$, and stationarity of either branch
pair in \eqref{eq:reduced_objective} therefore become polynomial equations
after denominators are cleared. Direct expansion gives degrees at most three,
four, and six, respectively. Identically zero equations are discarded; if an
active reduced objective is constant on a piece, its closure endpoints
represent all of its minimizers needed by the finite candidate set.

Sort the interval endpoints and all branch intersections. On each resulting
open interval, one branch realizes $a$ and one realizes $b$. The reduced
objective is a smooth rational function except where $a+b=1$. A minimum on the
closure of each piece is consequently attained at an endpoint, at that
transition, or at a stationary point. Taking the union over all branch pairs
and retaining roots that satisfy interval membership and branch activity gives
the finite superset in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}. Evaluating the original
four-corner objective filters extraneous roots introduced by denominator
clearing.
